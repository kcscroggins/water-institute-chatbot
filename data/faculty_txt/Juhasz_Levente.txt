Research Focus:
Levente Juhász is a computationally and technically oriented researcher whose work sits at the intersection of geospatial computing, visualization, spatial analytics, human-computer interaction (HCI), cartography, and data science. His research combines geographic information science (GIScience), visualization systems, interactive analytics, and computational spatial reasoning.

Major focus areas include:

1. Geospatial Visualization & Interactive Analytics: building systems that help humans understand, navigate, and reason about complex spatial data. This includes interactive maps, visual analytics systems, geospatial interfaces, and exploratory spatial analysis tools. His work addresses how humans cognitively interact with large-scale spatial information systems.

2. Human-Centered Spatial Computing: designing interfaces for human reasoning with spatial data, overlapping heavily with HCI, cognitive systems, visualization theory, and decision-support systems. Rather than purely technical GIS pipelines, his work focuses on making complex systems interpretable and usable.

3. Environmental & Urban Spatial Systems: spatial environmental systems, water-related geospatial analysis, urban-environment interactions, and environmental visualization systems. Geospatial analytics is central to watershed analysis, environmental monitoring, disaster resilience, infrastructure planning, and climate adaptation.

His Water Institute affiliation connects through geospatial approaches to water resource management, watershed analysis, environmental monitoring, and spatial decision-support for water systems.

His methodological areas include geospatial data systems, interactive visualization, web-based analytics, spatial machine learning, interface design, user-centered analytics, computational cartography, crowdsourced geographic data, and AI-driven geospatial analysis.
