Research Focus:
Joseph Delfino is a senior environmental engineering and water quality scholar with a long-standing career in environmental chemistry, water quality science, aquatic systems, pollution control, and environmental policy. He is one of the more traditional core water/environmental engineering researchers among the Water Institute affiliates.

His career spans academic research, environmental governance, public policy, engineering education, and scientific advisory leadership. He has also held major university leadership roles at UF, including serving as Interim President of the University of Florida and senior administrative positions in environmental engineering and graduate education.

Major focus areas include:

1. Water Quality & Aquatic Chemistry: chemical processes in aquatic environments, pollutant transport, nutrient cycling, and environmental contamination. Understanding how contaminants move through water systems, how ecosystems respond to pollution, and how engineering interventions improve water quality.

2. Environmental Pollution & Remediation: environmental contaminants, water pollution control, and remediation strategies including nutrient pollution, industrial contaminants, eutrophication, and ecosystem degradation. Especially important in Florida's wetlands, estuaries, groundwater systems, and freshwater ecosystems.

3. Environmental Engineering Systems: integrating chemistry, hydrology, ecology, infrastructure, and policy systems. Systems analysis approaches to environmental management, particularly in complex aquatic environments.

His methodological areas include aquatic chemical analysis, environmental monitoring, systems modeling, water quality assessment, pollutant transport analysis, environmental risk evaluation, ecosystem-scale analysis, regulatory science, and environmental policy.
