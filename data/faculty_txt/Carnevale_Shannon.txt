Research Focus:
Shannon Carnevale operates at the intersection of environmental education, conservation outreach, water resources, urban ecology, and applied natural resource management. Her role is strongly rooted in public engagement, extension programming, applied environmental communication, and community-based conservation.

Her work is representative of the UF/IFAS Extension mission: translating scientific knowledge into actionable practices for communities, landowners, and local governments.

Major focus areas include:

1. Extension-Based Environmental Science: taking environmental science and making it operational for the public through workshops, educational programming, outreach campaigns, and applied conservation training. She works with homeowners, municipalities, schools, environmental organizations, and community stakeholders.

2. Water Resource Education: water conservation, watershed protection, stormwater management, and freshwater stewardship. In Florida, these topics are especially important due to nutrient runoff, wetland loss, algal blooms, aquifer vulnerability, and rapid urbanization.

3. Urban & Residential Environmental Systems: human-environment interactions in rapidly developing areas, including landscaping impacts, fertilizer runoff, irrigation efficiency, invasive species, and sustainable residential practices.

Her activities include water conservation workshops, native landscaping education, community environmental stewardship programs, sustainable yard management training, public engagement campaigns, watershed awareness initiatives, urban water quality efforts, stormwater education, habitat conservation initiatives, and Florida-friendly landscaping programs.

Her Water Institute affiliation connects through practical water stewardship, environmental education, and public-facing conservation systems.
