Research Focus:
Her scholarship explores how literature and film represent nonhuman life, ecological systems, embodiment, species boundaries, and modernity's impact on humans and environments. A major throughline is the relationship between human identity and nonhuman worlds (animals, plants, environments, ecological systems). Her work sits at the intersection of literary criticism, environmental humanities, posthumanism, animal studies, and modern Italian culture.

Her affiliation with the UF Water Institute comes through environmental humanities, ecological storytelling, Anthropocene studies, and interdisciplinary environmental scholarship. She contributes a cultural and humanistic perspective to environmental questions.

Core research themes include:
- Ecocriticism: how literature and film engage with ecology, climate, environments, and environmental crises
- Posthumanism: challenges anthropocentrism, explores animal agency, interspecies entanglement, distributed subjectivity
- Animal Studies: human/animal boundaries, ethics of representation
- Critical Plant Studies: plant life, vegetal ontology, plant agency, ecological temporality
- Italian Modernism: reinterpreting authors (Italo Svevo, Carlo Emilio Gadda, Federigo Tozzi, Pier Paolo Pasolini) through embodiment, ecology, and animality
