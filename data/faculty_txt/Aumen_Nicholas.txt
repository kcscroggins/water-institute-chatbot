Research Focus:
Nicholas Aumen is an aquatic ecologist, wetlands scientist, environmental systems researcher, and science administrator with extensive experience in freshwater ecosystems and large-scale environmental restoration. His career strongly bridges federal environmental science, ecological monitoring, restoration management, and interdisciplinary environmental governance.

His major research themes include:

1. Everglades Ecosystem Science: hydrologic restoration, ecosystem degradation, biodiversity conservation, ecological recovery processes, and scientific contributions to the Comprehensive Everglades Restoration Plan (CERP).

2. Adaptive Ecosystem Management: integrating ecological science into management decisions, long-term ecological monitoring, uncertainty management, restoration evaluation, and science-policy coordination.

3. Large-Scale Environmental Systems: nutrient cycling, hydrologic connectivity, ecological thresholds, resilience, and ecosystem responses to environmental stressors.

His work is interdisciplinary, applied, systems-oriented, and management-relevant, emphasizing ecosystem-scale interactions, restoration implementation, and actionable environmental science. His methodological areas include field ecology, hydrologic analysis, environmental modeling, ecological monitoring systems, GIS/spatial ecology, ecosystem indicators, and long-term environmental datasets.

His affiliation with the Water Institute connects through freshwater systems, restoration ecology, hydrologic management, and interdisciplinary environmental systems science.
