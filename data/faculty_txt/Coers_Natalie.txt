Research Focus:
Natalie Coers works at the intersection of science communication, environmental communication, agricultural communication, public engagement, and natural resource education. Her research focuses on how people interpret scientific information, how environmental and agricultural issues are communicated, and how communication influences public understanding and decision-making.

Major focus areas include:

1. Environmental & Water Communication: communicating water-related issues to the public, stakeholder engagement, and environmental messaging effectiveness. This includes water conservation messaging, environmental literacy, agricultural-water conflicts, and communication around sustainability practices.

2. Science Communication & Public Trust: how scientific knowledge is interpreted by different audiences, including trust, framing, message design, media channels, and audience perception. Her work examines how communication strategies affect attitudes and behavior in environmental or agricultural contexts.

3. Stakeholder & Community Engagement: bridging gaps between experts and the public through extension systems, participatory communication, stakeholder engagement, and community-centered outreach.

Her Water Institute affiliation reflects work involving communication strategies around water resources, public engagement with conservation, and environmental education systems. Environmental outcomes often depend on whether people adopt recommendations, trust institutions, and coordinate behavior — her work addresses those human systems dimensions.

Her methodological areas include survey research, qualitative interviews, focus groups, communication theory, behavioral analysis, experimental messaging studies, public perception analysis, media analytics, and strategic messaging evaluation.
