Research Focus:
Mariola Ferraro is a microbiologist whose research centers on microbial ecology, environmental microbiology, microbial community interactions, and host-microbe/environment interactions. Her work operates at the interface of microbiology, ecosystems, environmental systems, and applied biological sciences.

Major focus areas include:

1. Microbial Communities as Complex Systems: how microbial communities organize, interact, adapt, and respond to environmental conditions. This includes microbial population dynamics, community structure, environmental perturbations, and ecological interactions. Modern microbiome science treats microbial systems as highly dynamic, networked, multi-agent biological ecosystems.

2. Environmental & Aquatic Microbiology: water-associated microbial systems, aquatic microbiomes, environmental microbial processes, and microbial impacts on ecosystem health. This includes freshwater microbial ecology, nutrient cycling, environmental resilience, contamination response, and ecosystem-associated microbial dynamics. Microbes are foundational to water quality, ecosystem functioning, and biogeochemical cycles.

3. Systems Biology & Ecological Interactions: systems biology, ecological network analysis, and complex adaptive biological systems including emergent behavior, interspecies interactions, ecological stability, and environmental adaptation.

Her Water Institute affiliation reflects the growing importance of microbes in water systems, aquatic ecosystem functioning, and environmental resilience. Microbial processes are essential to water quality, nutrient cycling, pollutant breakdown, and ecosystem recovery.

Her methodological areas include microbiome sequencing, microbial community analysis, metagenomics, molecular ecology, bioinformatics, microbial network analysis, and environmental sampling.
