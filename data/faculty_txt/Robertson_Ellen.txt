Research Focus:
Ellen Robertson's work sits at the intersection of wildlife ecology, conservation science, ecosystem dynamics, environmental systems, and aquatic/wetland ecology. Her research focuses on how ecological systems function under environmental stress, species-environment interactions, and conservation within changing landscapes.

Major focus areas include:

1. Ecological Systems & Species Interactions: how species interact with ecosystems, how environmental change alters ecological relationships, and how ecosystems maintain resilience. This includes habitat structure, population dynamics, ecosystem responses, and conservation outcomes. Her work treats ecosystems as interconnected adaptive systems rather than isolated species problems.

2. Conservation Under Environmental Stress: ecosystem degradation, habitat fragmentation, climate pressures, hydrologic changes, and biodiversity threats. This places her within resilience science, sustainability, and adaptive ecosystem management.

3. Aquatic & Wetland Ecosystems: freshwater ecosystems, wetland ecology, watershed systems, and hydrologically sensitive habitats. Florida ecosystems involve strong coupling between hydrology, habitat structure, biodiversity, and ecosystem stability, making water systems foundational to wildlife conservation in the region.

Her Water Institute affiliation connects because water systems are deeply connected to habitat quality, biodiversity, species distribution, and ecosystem resilience. She contributes from the perspective of biological systems, ecosystem interactions, and conservation dynamics within water-influenced environments.

Her methodological areas include ecological field studies, spatial ecology, biodiversity monitoring, ecosystem modeling, population analysis, habitat assessment, GIS/spatial analytics, species distribution modeling, connectivity analysis, and conservation data systems.
